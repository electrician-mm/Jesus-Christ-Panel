Who Is Jesus Christ

According to the Holy Bible

The Holy Bible presents Jesus Christ as the eternal Son of God, at once fully divine and fully human. He is the long-promised Messiah and the one who came into the world to offer forgiveness and eternal life.

God Made Known in Human Form

The opening of the Gospel of John describes Him as the Word who was with God and who was God, and who “became flesh and dwelt among us” (John 1:1–14). Throughout the New Testament He is called the Son of God. He entered human history through the virgin Mary in Bethlehem, sharing fully in human life while remaining without sin.

The Promised Messiah

The title “Christ” means “Anointed One.” The New Testament understands Jesus as the fulfillment of the Old Testament’s hope for a coming king, prophet, and servant (Isaiah 7:14; 9:6–7; 53; Micah 5:2). He accepted this identity, and those closest to Him recognized Him as the one long awaited.

The Savior

The heart of the biblical witness is that Jesus willingly gave His life on the cross as a sacrifice for sin, rose from the dead on the third day, and ascended to heaven. Through faith in Him, the Bible teaches that people receive forgiveness and the gift of eternal life (John 3:16; Romans 10:9; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8).

His Titles and Work

He is spoken of as Lord, the Good Shepherd, the Way, the Truth, and the Life, the one through whom all things were made, and the one who continues to intercede for His people (John 14:6; Colossians 1:15–17; Hebrews 1).

In this way the Holy Bible portrays Jesus not simply as a teacher or moral example, but as God the Son who entered the world so that people might be reconciled to God. These claims rest on the witness of both the Old and New Testaments.
